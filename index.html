✦ Welcome

Journaling: A Voyage to the Interior

So, you've decided to embark on the adventure of journaling. Welcome to the club — and what a fine club it is.

Journaling is more than scribbling thoughts onto paper. It is a form of self-expression, a conversation with your innermost self, and a chance to become the author of your own life story. Whether you're a seasoned wordsmith or someone who hasn't written a page since school, the practice meets you exactly where you are.

"The only journal that matters is the one you write."
— Joan Didion

What exactly is journaling?

Journaling is the practice of regularly recording thoughts, feelings, experiences, and observations — in a structured or unstructured format. It serves as a personal outlet for self-reflection, creativity, and emotional expression.

Think of it as having your own personal therapist, confidante, and time capsule — all wrapped in a single notebook.

Words to carry with you

  • "Journaling is a way of tapping into your subconscious and accessing your intuition." — Julia Cameron
  • "Writing is a way of talking without being interrupted." — Jules Renard
  • "Fill your paper with the breathings of your heart." — William Wordsworth

Remember: there are no right or wrong ways to journal. This is the one place in life where you are completely free.

✦ Overview

Types of Journaling

Journaling is not one-size-fits-all. Each style serves a different purpose — try a few to discover what resonates with you.

🌒

Reflective

Processing daily experiences, emotions, and lessons learned. The most classic form.

🌻

Gratitude

Intentionally noting things you are grateful for — shifts your attention toward abundance.

🔵

Bullet Journal

A customisable hybrid of planner, diary, and to-do list. Great for organising minds.

🎨

Creative

Combining writing with drawings, doodles, and collages. No artistic skill required.

🌙

Dream

Recording dreams upon waking to explore patterns, symbols, and the subconscious.

✈️

Travel

Documenting experiences, places, flavours, and feelings while you explore the world.

"I never travel without my diary. One should always have something sensational to read on the train."
— Oscar Wilde
✦ Method

Digital Journaling

Digital journaling means keeping your journal in a digital format — on an app, a document, or a dedicated platform. It suits those who type faster than they write, or who value searchability and portability.

Advantages

  • Accessibility: Write from any device, anywhere in the world.
  • Searchability: Find a memory from three years ago in seconds.
  • Organisation: Tags, folders, and categories keep everything tidy.
  • Multimedia: Attach photos, voice notes, or links alongside your words.
  • Backup: Cloud storage means your journal can never burn or flood.

Recommended Tools

  • Day One — A beautifully designed app built purely for journaling, with prompts and daily reminders.
  • Notion — Highly customisable with templates. Perfect for those who love structure.
  • Obsidian — Ideal for writers who want to link thoughts and build a personal knowledge base.
  • Google Docs — Simple, free, and always accessible. Nothing fancy required.
  • Evernote — Strong for capturing mixed media and organising across topics.
The best journaling app is the one you actually open.
✦ Method

Manual — Written Journaling

There is something irreplaceable about pen on paper. Written journaling slows the mind, deepens reflection, and makes the act of recording feel deliberate and sacred.

Why write by hand?

  • Intimacy: Your handwriting is uniquely yours — no font can replicate it.
  • Mindfulness: The slower pace encourages deeper thought.
  • Memory: Studies suggest writing by hand improves recall and comprehension.
  • Freedom: Draw a margin note, doodle a feeling, cross things out boldly.
  • No distractions: No notifications. No tabs. Just you and the page.

What to write in

  • A plain notebook — The most accessible and freeing option.
  • A Leuchtturm1917 — Beloved by bullet journalers for its numbered pages.
  • A Moleskine — Classic, portable, and satisfying in the hand.
  • A gratitude journal — Often pre-structured with daily prompts.
  • A sketchbook — If you want maximum visual freedom.
"A journal is a friend who keeps your secrets and never judges you."
✦ Method

Visual Journaling

Visual journaling combines words with drawings, photographs, collages, paint, and imagery. It is for those who feel that language alone cannot capture the fullness of an experience.

Why go visual?

  • Beyond words: Some emotions are better drawn than described.
  • No pressure: There is no "correct" way to make art in a personal journal.
  • Healing: Art therapy studies show visual expression can process trauma and grief.
  • Creativity: Regular visual journaling loosens creative blocks in other areas of life.
  • Reflection: Looking back through a visual journal is a richer experience than reading words.

Where to begin

  • Art journals with thick pages that handle paint and glue
  • Mixed media combining watercolour, magazine clippings, and handwriting
  • Scrapbooking — tickets, receipts, packaging, anything that marks a moment
  • Simple doodling in the margins of your existing notebook
"Creativity is not a talent. It is a way of operating." — John Cleese
✦ Getting Started

How Do I Actually Begin?

"Just start" is the most common advice — and the least useful one. Here is a gentler, more practical path.

01

Set your minimum

Choose something so small it feels impossible to fail: 2 minutes, 5 lines, or one paragraph. Consistency matters more than length. Even a voice note counts.

02

Attach it to a trigger

Habits stick when linked to something you already do. Try: after fajr, after breakfast, after dinner, or right before bed. "After I make my tea, I'll write for 2 minutes."

03

Use a simple template

On blank-page days, use this: (1) What happened? (2) How did I feel? (3) Why might I feel that? (4) One gratitude. (5) One intention for tomorrow.

04

Try brain dumping

Set a timer for 3–5 minutes. Write anything. No grammar, no structure — just empty the open tabs in your mind. Stop when the timer ends.

Quick-start prompts

  • Right now, my mind is full of…
  • The best part of today was…
  • I'm avoiding ___ because…
  • Something I need to hear today is…
  • If I could redo one moment this week, it would be…

7-Day Starter Plan

  1. Day 1: Why do I want to journal?
  2. Day 2: What's weighing on me lately?
  3. Day 3: Three things I'm grateful for and why
  4. Day 4: What do I need more of in my life?
  5. Day 5: A moment I'm quietly proud of
  6. Day 6: What I'm learning about myself
  7. Day 7: A letter to my future self
If you miss a day, the rule is simple: never miss twice. Two lines tomorrow is enough.
✦ Content

What Should I Write?

There is no should when it comes to journaling. Write as disjointed or organised as your mind feels in this moment. Here are some directions when you need them:

  • Daily reflection — What happened, what you felt, what you noticed.
  • Gratitude log — Name three things, and write one sentence about each.
  • Goal mapping — Where do you want to be in 3 months? What's blocking you?
  • Conversations you didn't have — Write the thing you couldn't say out loud.
  • Letters to yourself — Past you, future you, or present you on a hard day.
  • Things that made you smile — Small joys are worth recording too.
  • Questions you're sitting with — You don't need to answer them. Just write them.
"The more grateful I am, the more beauty I see." — Mary Davis

When you don't know where to start

Open to a blank page and write: "I don't know what to write today, but…" and see what follows. Something always does.

✦ The Why

Benefits of Journaling

The case for journaling is backed by both psychological research and centuries of human testimony. Here is what the practice can genuinely offer.

  • 🪞
    Self-AwarenessWriting reveals patterns in your thinking and behaviour you'd otherwise miss. Over time, you come to know yourself with remarkable depth.
  • Stress ReductionExternalising worries onto the page reduces their mental weight. The act of naming a feeling begins to defuse it.
  • Improved Mental HealthResearch in expressive writing (Dr James Pennebaker) shows measurable reduction in anxiety and depressive symptoms through regular writing.
  • Creative ExpansionJournaling loosens the inner critic and opens a channel to creative thinking. Many writers, artists, and thinkers credit their journals as their real work.
  • Clarity on GoalsWriting down what you want — and regularly revisiting it — dramatically increases the chance of achieving it.
  • 📖
    A Living ArchiveYour journal becomes a record of who you are: the fears, the joys, the ordinary Tuesdays. Future you will thank present you for every page.

✦ Ready-to-Use

Journaling Templates

Pick a structure that fits your mood and make it yours.

Daily Templates

The Daily Check-In

A gentle five-minute ritual for every morning or evening. Simple, grounding, consistent.

Today's Date ___ How am I feeling right now? (one word or more)...
What happened today?...
One thing I'm grateful for...
One intention for tomorrow...
5 min Daily Beginner

Morning Pages

Julia Cameron's classic — three pages of unfiltered stream-of-consciousness writing, first thing.

No template. Just write. Open the notebook. Write anything.
"I'm sleepy, I want chai, I don't know what—"
Keep going until three pages are done.
20–30 min Morning Stream

Brain Dump

Set a timer. Empty your mind completely. No grammar, no structure. Just out.

Set timer: 5 minutes Write everything that's taking up space...
Worries, lists, random thoughts, feelings...
Stop when the timer rings. Done.
5 min Anytime Anxiety

Reflective Templates

Weekly Review

A Sunday ritual to close the week with awareness and open the next with intention.

Week of ___ What went well this week?...
What was difficult?...
What did I learn about myself?...
Three priorities for next week...
15 min Weekly Growth

Gratitude Deep Dive

Go beyond the list. Write three things and actually explain why you're grateful.

Today I'm grateful for... 1. ___ because ___...
2. ___ because ___...
3. ___ because ___...
How do these make me feel?
10 min Gratitude Positivity

Letter to Future Self

Write a letter to yourself six months or a year from now. Seal it. Reread it later.

Dear Future Me, Right now I am feeling...
I hope by the time you read this...
What I most want you to remember is...
With love, Past You.
20 min Reflection Perspective

Emotional & Healing Templates

Processing a Hard Day

For when something happened and you need to untangle it before sleep.

What happened? What did I feel in my body?...
What story am I telling myself about it?...
Is that story definitely true?...
What do I need right now?
10 min Healing Emotions

The Unsent Letter

Write the thing you couldn't say — to anyone, about anything. You never have to send it.

Dear ___, What I've been wanting to say is...
What I feel when I think of you is...
What I wish you knew is...
What I'm letting go of is...
Open-ended Release Healing
